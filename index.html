Quick safari FAQ reference. Open a section, then tap a question to view the answer.

General Information
Do I need a visa to enter Tanzania?

Visa requirements vary by passport. We recommend arranging your Tanzania visa before travel whenever possible. Keep your passport, visa confirmation, and entry documents accessible while traveling.

What should I do if my flight is delayed?

Contact us as soon as possible if your arrival changes. When we know about a delay, we can adjust your airport greeting, transfers, and onward arrangements.

Will a guide or representative meet me at the airport?

Yes. A Prominent Africa representative will meet you after you clear customs and collect your luggage. Please keep us updated if your flight details change.

What should I do if my luggage arrives late or is lost?

Complete the airline’s delayed or lost luggage report before leaving the airport. Share the claim information with us, and we will help coordinate delivery once your luggage arrives.

Can the daily schedule change?

Yes. Steve may adjust timing and routing for wildlife, weather, road conditions, or flight updates while protecting the overall journey.

What luggage works best for safari?

Soft-sided luggage is best for safari vehicles and bush flights. Auric Air currently lists a 20 kg allowance per adult, inclusive of hand luggage.

Will Wi-Fi and mobile service be available?

Wi-Fi is available at many accommodations, but speed and reliability vary. Mobile coverage is strongest near towns and can be limited in remote safari areas.

Money Matters
Which currency is accepted?

Tanzanian shillings are useful for small local purchases. U.S. dollars are widely accepted at hotels, safari lodges, camps, and many tourism businesses.

Should I carry cash?

Yes. Carry a sensible amount of cash in smaller denominations for gratuities, small purchases, and locations where card terminals or internet service may be unavailable.

Will credit cards work?

Major cards are generally accepted at larger hotels, lodges, and resorts. Processing fees may apply, and service can be interrupted by weak connectivity.

Are older U.S. dollar notes accepted?

Bring clean, undamaged U.S. dollar notes issued in 2009 or later. Older, marked, or torn notes may be refused.

Safety & Health Matters
What should I expect on game drives?

Roads can be dusty, uneven, and bumpy. Use the vehicle handholds, follow Steve’s instructions, and tell him whenever you need a comfort stop.

How should I manage prescription medication?

Carry enough medication for the full journey, keep it in original packaging, and pack it in your carry-on luggage.

How do we stay safe around wildlife?

Remain inside the vehicle unless Steve confirms an area is safe, never feed wildlife, and follow camp escort rules after dark.

What about sun and hydration?

Use high-SPF sunscreen, wear a hat, and drink water regularly. The sun can be strong even when temperatures feel comfortable.

What should I do if I feel unwell?

Tell Steve immediately. He can help assess the situation, contact the lodge team, and arrange medical assistance when needed.

Should I drink tap water?

Use sealed bottled or properly filtered water for drinking and brushing your teeth unless your accommodation specifically confirms its tap water is safe.

💵 Gratuities & Tipping

Gratuities are an important part of the hospitality and tourism industry throughout East Africa and are a meaningful way to recognize exceptional service. While tipping is always at your discretion, it is customary and greatly appreciated.

East Africa Safari

Our Recommendation
Private Safari Guide: $10–25 USD per person, per day
Lodge or Camp Staff: $20 USD per person, per day, shared tip box
Hotel Staff: $5 USD per person, per day
Private Airport or Hotel Transfer: $5 USD per vehicle

Additional Tipping Situations

Airport porter: $2 per bag. Hotel bell staff: $2 per bag. Housekeeping: $1–2 per room, per day. Restaurant server: 5–10% when no service charge is included. Taxi driver: round up or add $2–5 for exceptional help. Spa or local activity staff: $2–5.

Additional Guidance

Bring newer U.S. dollar bills in good condition, preferably 2009 or later. Carry $1, $5, $10, and $20 bills. Cash is generally preferred. “Asante” means “thank you” in Swahili. When uncertain, ask us.

Above all, gratuities should reflect your personal experience. Exceptional service may inspire additional recognition, and every gratuity is a meaningful expression of appreciation.
